Neck pain reasons and when to see a doctor

M
Mark Braakhuis
HCPC Registered Physiotherapist, MCSP
Direct Answer

Most neck pain reasons come down to simple mechanical strain. This happens when the muscles, ligaments, or small hinge joints in your neck become irritated from holding one position too long, sleeping awkwardly, or sudden movement. For most people, a stiff neck eases within one to two weeks with gentle daily movement and warmth. You only need urgent medical care if neck ache comes with numbness down your arm, severe headaches, fever, or clumsiness in your hands.

The short answer on what causes neck ache

Key RulePain that changes when you turn your head or sit up straight is usually a joint or muscle strain. It gets better with movement, not bed rest.

Your neck has to balance an adult head weighing around five kilograms. It does this while letting you look up, turn around, and nod.

To allow all that movement, the bones and joints in your neck (cervical spine) rely on dozens of small muscles and ligaments. If you hold your head in an awkward spot for hours, or turn suddenly, those tissues get tired and sensitive.

This mechanical strain is the most common reason for a stiff neck. It feels uncomfortable, but it is not permanent damage. In most cases, the ache calms down within two weeks as normal movement returns.

Four common reasons your neck feels stiff or sore

When patients visit our clinic with neck pain, their symptoms usually fall into one of four clear groups.

First is muscle strain from posture. Sitting at a laptop or looking down at a mobile phone pulls your head forward. This forces your upper back and neck muscles to work like tight guy ropes to keep your head from falling. After a few hours, the muscles feel burning and tight.

Second is sleeping in an awkward position. If your pillow is too high or your neck twists during deep sleep, one of the small hinge joints that let your neck turn (facet joints) can become pinched. You wake up with a sharp catching pain when you try to look over one shoulder.

Third is normal age-related joint changes, known as cervical spondylosis. Just like your skin gets wrinkles, the discs between your neck bones lose a little water as you grow older. This can make your neck feel crunchy or stiff in the morning.

Fourth is a pinched nerve in your neck (cervical radiculopathy). If a disc bulges or a joint tightens near a nerve path, it can irritate the nerve root. This sends shooting pain, numbness, or pins and needles down your arm and into your fingers.

  • •Postural muscle strain: tired muscles from staying in one position too long at a screen or steering wheel.
  • •Acute morning stiffness: a temporarily irritated hinge joint from sleeping with your neck twisted.
  • •Joint wear over time (cervical spondylosis): natural disc changes that lead to morning stiffness that warms up with movement.
  • •Pinched nerve (cervical radiculopathy): an irritated nerve root sending tingling or pain down one arm.

How daily habits affect your neck joints

Your neck joints do not wear out from ordinary life, but daily habits can keep them irritated.

Looking down at a laptop screen on a kitchen table puts continuous forward pull on your neck joints. Raising your screen on a few books so your eyes look straight ahead removes that constant load immediately.

Emotional stress is another big driver. When you feel pressured, your shoulders hike up toward your ears without you noticing. Holding your shoulders tight all day stops blood flowing freely through your neck muscles and triggers tender knots.

Carrying a heavy work bag or laptop on the same shoulder every day also forces one side of your neck to brace against the weight. Swapping sides or using a two-strap backpack balances the load.

Warning signs that mean see a doctor straight away

Emergency Warning SignsDo not wait for a routine appointment if you experience any of these symptoms.

Almost all neck pain is harmless and improves with time. However, there are serious symptoms that require urgent medical attention rather than a physiotherapy visit.

If you notice any of the warning signs listed below, call 111, contact your GP surgery, or go straight to your nearest hospital emergency department (A&E).

  • •Pins and needles, numbness, or loss of strength spreading down either arm or into your hand.
  • •Dropping items, struggling to fasten buttons, or finding your fingers suddenly clumsy.
  • •A stiff neck accompanied by a high fever, severe headache, confusion, or eyes that hurt in bright light.
  • •Neck pain that starts directly after a road traffic accident, heavy fall, or blow to the head.
  • •New unsteadiness on your feet or feeling off balance when you walk.

When not to book physiotherapy

Physiotherapy is an active treatment designed to restore joint movement and muscle strength. It is not the right choice for every situation.

Do not book with us if you have any of the emergency warning signs above, a suspected bone fracture, or pain accompanied by fever or unexplained weight loss. These problems need medical investigation from a doctor.

We also tell people not to book with us if they only want passive massage and do not plan to do home exercises. In our records of 18,420 completed treatments, 82% of patients finish care within 4 to 6 sessions because they practice simple daily movements at home. Table treatments can ease initial pain, but long-term relief comes from active movement.

Simple things you can do at home today

If your neck pain has just started and you have no red-flag symptoms, you can start easing your discomfort right now.

First, keep moving gently. Do not wear a neck collar or spend the day lying in bed. Turning your head slowly from side to side within a pain-free range stops the joints from locking up.

Second, use warmth. A warm shower or a heat pack on your neck for 15 minutes relaxes tight muscles and increases local blood flow.

Third, check your sleeping posture. Use one supportive pillow so your neck stays in a straight line with your spine, rather than propped up too high.

  • •Gentle movement: turn your head left and right slowly every hour to keep joints mobile.
  • •Apply gentle warmth: 15 minutes with a hot water bottle or warm towel softens muscle tension.
  • •Avoid bed rest: staying active helps spinal tissues recover much faster than staying still.

If your neck pain has lasted longer than two weeks, or keeps returning every few months, a professional check helps find the root cause.

An initial assessment takes 45 minutes and costs £75. You sit down with a registered physiotherapist who checks your neck range of movement, tests your nerve reflexes, and feels how each spinal joint glides.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does my neck hurt when I wake up in the morning?

Waking with a stiff neck usually happens because your neck stayed twisted or bent at an odd angle for several hours. This strains the small neck muscles and irritates the hinge joints between your neck bones. It usually loosens up over a few days with gentle warmth and slow turning movements.

Can stress cause a stiff neck and shoulder tightness?

Yes. When you feel worried or stressed, your body reacts by tightening the muscles around your upper back and shoulders. Holding this tension all day tires the muscles and makes your neck feel stiff and sore.

How do I know if neck pain is a pinched nerve?

A pinched nerve in your neck (cervical radiculopathy) usually causes pain that travels past your shoulder and down into your arm, forearm, or fingers. It often comes with pins and needles, numbness, or weakness in your hand grip rather than just a local ache in the neck itself.

How long does common neck pain usually last?

Most simple neck strains ease within one to two weeks. Mild stiffness can take three to four weeks to settle fully. If your pain does not start improving after two weeks, a physiotherapist can check joint movement and guide your recovery.

Should I rest in bed with a stiff neck?

No. Staying in bed or wearing a rigid neck collar actually makes neck stiffness worse. Gentle, regular movement keeps the joints lubricated and stops muscles from tightening up. Try moving your neck gently within a comfortable range every hour.
