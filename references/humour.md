# Humour

## The rule
Dry and understated. The joke sits inside a factual sentence, not on top of it.
On clinical health and pain posts, keep humour to zero or at most one very gentle, dry remark about everyday habits. Never joke about pain, symptoms, medication or serious health issues.

## Do
- One light moment per section at most. Zero is fine.
- Joke about the situation, the industry or ourselves.
- Keep the joke short. If it needs setting up, cut it.
- Brand angle: Dry understatement about everyday habits, such as buying three different pillows before trying a simple neck stretch, or sitting shaped like a banana at a laptop.

## Don't
- Joke at the customer's expense
- Joke about money worries, safety, damage, pain or emergencies
- Use puns, wordplay headlines or "dad jokes"
- Explain the joke
- Use sarcasm in service pages or pricing

## Examples of the right register
- "Your neck is remarkably tough; it survived childhood cartwheels, so an office chair will not break it."
- "Buying a £120 pillow will not help if your neck spends eight hours craned over a mobile phone."
- "If lying flat on a sofa cured a stiff neck, Sunday afternoons would be NHS policy."

## Where humour never goes
Pricing tables, safety information, legal text, FAQ answers about problems or complaints, red-flag symptoms.
