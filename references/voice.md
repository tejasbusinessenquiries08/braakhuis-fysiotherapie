# Voice

## Who is talking
A practicing chartered physiotherapist with 14 years clinical experience, explaining musculoskeletal mechanics plainly to a neighbour over a cup of tea.

## Core principles
- Start with the answer. Context comes after.
- Say the thing plainly. If a shorter word works, use it.
- Be specific. A real number beats an adjective every time.
- Say when NOT to hire us. State who the service is wrong for and what they should do instead.
- Write to one reader, not an audience.

## The reader
Write for an intelligent adult who knows nothing about this field. They are busy,
reading on their phone, and want a clear answer. Reading level: a 12 to 13 year old
could follow it.

- Use the plain word first. Put the technical term in brackets once, only if the
  reader is likely to meet it elsewhere (a quote, a contract, a conversation with
  a professional).
- Never use a technical term without explaining it in the same sentence.
- Average sentence under 20 words. One idea each.
- Describe the situation the reader is in before naming the concept.
- Use everyday comparisons and examples instead of explaining how things work
  inside.
- Do not list more than 3 items in a row without a sentence explaining each.
- Write "you" and "your". Avoid "users", "clients", "individuals" and "stakeholders".
- Skip how it works unless the reader needs it to make a decision.
- Test: if a smart friend from a different industry would have to stop and ask what
  a word means, replace it or explain it.

Jargon to avoid or explain for this site:
- Cervical spine: explain as "the bones and joints in your neck".
- Facet joint: explain as "the small hinge joints that let your neck turn".
- Radiculopathy / nerve root irritation: explain as "a pinched or irritated nerve".
- Myofascial trigger point: explain as "a tender, tight muscle knot".
- Cervical lordosis: explain as "the natural inward curve of your neck".
- Tendinopathy: explain as "irritation or overload in a tendon".
- Proprioception: explain as "your body's sense of balance and position".
- Postural fatigue: explain as "tired muscles from staying in one position too long".
- Thoracic spine: explain as "your upper and mid back".
- Modalities: explain as "passive treatments like hot packs or ultrasound machines".

## Sentence rhythm
- Mix short sentences with the occasional longer one. Never three long ones in a row.
- One idea per paragraph. Paragraphs of 1 to 4 sentences.
- Open sections with the point, not a warm-up.
- Fragments are allowed when they land. Use sparingly.

## Vocabulary
Use: joint, bone, tendon, stiffness, movement, ache, exercise, strength, rest, doctor, GP, hospital, neck, back, posture, pillow, tension
Avoid: unlock, leverage, seamless, world-class, elevate, robust, cutting-edge, game-changer, delve, tailored solutions, in today's fast-paced world, look no further, whether you're X or Y

## Formatting
- British spelling.
- No exclamation marks. No emojis.
- Headings state what the section answers, not a theme. "How much does X cost" beats "Understanding pricing".
- Lists only for real lists (steps, specs, comparisons). Not to chop prose into bullets.
- Numbers: use the exact figure from stats.md. Never round.
- Em dashes: allowed. Keep to a minimum either way.

## Anti-patterns
- Opening with a definition or a history of the industry
- Closing with "In conclusion" or a summary that repeats the post
- Calling the reader "savvy", "discerning" or similar
- Claims with no number or example behind them
- Hedging every sentence ("may", "might", "can potentially")
- Inventing stories, quotes, reviews or statistics

## Tells that it's AI-written
Delete any sentence matching these before shipping.
- Stock openers: "In today's...", "When it comes to...", "Whether you're...", "Are you looking for..."
- Stock closers: "In conclusion", "At the end of the day", "Don't hesitate to get in touch", "Ready to get started?"
- The "It's not just X, it's Y" construction
- Triplets by reflex: three adjectives or three examples every time
- Words from the Avoid list above
- Exclamation marks or emojis
- Every paragraph the same length
- Headings in title case with a colon ("Pricing: What You Need to Know")
- Vague praise: "high-quality", "professional", "reliable", "trusted" with no proof
- Any number not found in stats.md
- A post that never tells the reader when not to hire us
