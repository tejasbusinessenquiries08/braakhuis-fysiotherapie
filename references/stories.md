### The runner who needed calf strength, not ultrasound
- **What happened:** A 38-year-old marathon runner came in after 3 months of recurring Achilles pain. She had spent 8 weeks receiving therapeutic ultrasound and ice baths elsewhere without improvement. We measured her single-leg calf raise capacity: she could manage 11 repetitions on the injured side versus 28 on the healthy side. We stopped the passive modalities and started a progressive heavy-slow loading protocol. Within 6 weeks, her symmetry returned to 94% and she ran her half-marathon without pain.
- **The numbers:** 11 reps versus 28 reps initially, 6 weeks of loading, 94% strength symmetry reached.
- **The point:** Tendons require measured mechanical load to remodel. Passive machines feel soothing but do not rebuild tendon tissue tolerance.
- **Use it in posts about:** Running injuries, Achilles tendinopathy, passive versus active treatment.
- **Do not use it in:** Acute trauma or post-operative fractures.
